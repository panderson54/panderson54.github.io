What Homebase tracks

Everything about the house.
Nowhere else to look.

Built for a single household, not a property-management suite. The model number, the filter size, who fixed it last time, and when it's due again.

Maintenance Dashboard

Overdue, due soon, and up to date across the whole house. Mark a task done and the next due date recomputes itself. Pro service visits are flagged separately.

Appliance Inventory

Make, model, serial, install date, manuals, and rating-plate photos. Enter a model number or snap the nameplate and Claude fills in the details it's confident about.

Templates & Consumables

Add a furnace and its usual checklist comes with it: filter changes, cleanings, a pro tune-up interval. Filters, anode rods, and other consumables get part numbers and purchase links.

Vendors & Quotes

Every contractor, their rating, and every visit they've made. Log competing quotes against a job, compare them side by side, and accept one to decline the rest.

Rooms, Zones & Paint

The house itself: square footage, year built, floor plans, and inspection reports. Every paint color by room, with the manufacturer code and a real swatch, for the next touch-up.

AI-Ready Context

One click exports the whole house as Markdown sized for an LLM's context window. A built-in MCP server lets an AI agent look things up and log service visits for you.

Your house, behind your door.

Homebase is self-hosted on a server you control, so the details of your home stay with you. Sign-in uses Google OAuth with a strict allowlist: every page, upload, and file needs a Google account whose email is on the list. No passwords, no self-serve sign-up. Remove an address and restart, and that person is locked out immediately, even mid-session.

Getting started

Up and running
in an afternoon.

Fork the repo and follow the README. It's a Flask app backed by SQLite, with a Docker Compose setup if you'd rather not touch Python at all.

1

Fork & deploy

Run it with Docker Compose, or under uvicorn and systemd behind Nginx on a Raspberry Pi or any Linux server. Migrations run automatically, so updates are pull, rebuild, restart.

2

Set up sign-in

Create a Google OAuth client, then list the email addresses allowed in. The first allowed sign-in creates your household, and everyone on the list shares it.

3

Add your appliances

Walk the house once. Each appliance picks up its category's maintenance checklist, and from then on the dashboard tells you what needs doing and when.
